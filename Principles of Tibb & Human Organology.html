Organogenesis Tibb Three-Organ Embryology

The Germ Layers & Primary Differentiation

"Akhlat (humors) are proximate principles of the human body, however the more proximate part is Az'a-e-basit (cells & tissues). In addition, Az'a-e-aliyah (organs) are composed of Az'a-e-basit." — Ali Ibn al-Abbas.

Internal Layer Hot & Moist

Endoderm

Identified as the inner layer, growing into the lining of most of the gastrointestinal tract, lungs, liver, pancreas, and urogenital tract.

Derived Organs & Cells:
  • Pancreatic Cells
  • Alveolar Cells (Lungs)
  • Thyroid Cells & GI Mucosa
  • Liver & Gallbladder Epithelium
Tibb Dominant Quality: Heat (Liver-dominant)
Middle Layer Dry & Hot

Mesoderm

The middle layer growing into dermis, heart, muscles, urogenital system, bones, and bone marrow. Dominant dryness quality associated with heart force.

Derived Organs & Cells:
  • Cardiac, Skeletal & Smooth Muscle
  • Red Blood Cells
  • Tubule Cells of Kidney
  • Bones & Connective Tendons
Tibb Dominant Quality: Dryness (Heart-dominant)
External Layer Dominant Moistness

Ectoderm

The outer layer maturing into the central and peripheral nervous system, skin epidermis, hair, sense organs, and precursor mammary glands.

Derived Organs & Cells:
  • Skin Epidermis Cells
  • Neurons & Brain Cells
  • Pigment Cells (Melanocytes)
  • Sensory Receptors & Hair
Tibb Dominant Quality: Moistness (Brain-dominant)
Tissue Classification Histology & Qualitative Balance

Four Primary Tissues & Their Temperaments

Cells sharing mutual embryonic origin form four broad categories of tissues. The temperament of each derived organ is governed by tissue ratio, blood supply, and mechanical movement.

Cold & Dry

Connective Tissue

Provides binding, protection, support, and integration. Includes bone, cartilage, dense/loose tissue, adipose, and blood.

Examples: Cartilage, Bone, Tendons, Adipose.
Dry & Hot

Muscular Tissue

Excitable in nature, responding to movement through contraction and relaxation. Consists of skeletal, smooth, and cardiac muscles.

Examples: Heart muscle, Biceps, GI tract wall.
Hot & Dry to Hot & Moist

Epithelial Tissue

Sheets of cells covering exterior surfaces, lining internal cavities, and forming glands. Includes squamous, cuboidal, columnar, and transitional.

Examples: Epidermis, Intestinal lining, Bladder.
Moist & Hot to Cold & Moist

Nervous Tissue

Excitable due to electrochemical signal propagation. Nerve impulses communicate between all bodily regions (Soma, Axon, Dendrites).

Examples: Brain, Spinal Cord, Peripheral Nerves.

3 Key Factors Determining Organ Temperament:

1
Qualitative Ratio: The proportional balance or ratio of different tissue types contained within the organ.
2
Blood Supply Ratio: The volume of vascularization and blood flow supplying heat and moistness to tissues.
3
Movement & Activity: Mechanical contraction, friction, or work executed by organ tissues generating heat and dryness.

Taxonomy Structure, Faculties & Powers

Classification of Organs

In Tibb literature, organs are classified through three distinct frameworks based on structural composition, innately assigned faculties, and interactive power transfer (Table 17).

Simple Organs

Aa'za-e-mufridah / Basit

Homogeneous organs where any visible part carries the same name, structure, and definition as the organ as a whole.

Cartilage Ligament Tendon Membrane Fat Muscle Nerve Bone

Compound Organs

Aa'za-e-aliyah / Murakbah

Heterogeneous instruments composed of multiple simpler tissue parts, differing in nature and name from the whole unit.

Brain Liver Heart Stomach Kidneys Pancreas Intestines Hands & Feet

Governing Triad Integrated Energy & Information Exchange

Heart, Liver & Brain Theory of Organs

The principal players in the body are the Heart, Liver, and Brain. Completely integrated in energy transfer and information supply; imbalance in their connection leads to physical or psychical disease (Abu Sahil Mashi). Modern medicine correlates these with the Immune, Endocrine, and Nervous systems.

Vital Group (Qalb)

The Heart

Center of vital force and powerful symbol. Aristotle posited the heart as the supreme seat situated in the body's center. Controls the cardiovascular and respiratory systems.

Individual Preservation: Center of vital force
Auxiliary Organs: Lungs, Arteries
Modern Equivalent: Immune & Cardiac System
Metabolic Group (Jigar)

The Liver

Center of nutrition and humors production. Galen emphasized the liver as a primary organ of the body due to its prominent size and metabolic generation.

Individual Preservation: Center of nutrition
Auxiliary Organs: Stomach, Gallbladder, Veins
Modern Equivalent: Endocrine & Exocrine System
Psychic Group (Dimagh)

The Brain

Control center of thoughts, emotions, sensation, and movement (Hippocrates/Galen). Reigned over central and peripheral nervous systems.

Individual Preservation: Sensation & Movement
Auxiliary Organs: Nerves, Spinal Cord
Modern Equivalent: Central Nervous System

Principal Organs (Aa'za Ra'eesa)

Absolutely necessary for life of individual and race. Centers of primary functions.

  • Individual Preservation: Heart (vital force), Liver (nutrition), Brain (sensation/movement).
  • Race Preservation: Heart, Liver, Brain, and Testicles/Ovaries (center of reproduction).

Auxiliary Organs (Aa'za Khadima)

Divided into two servant types:

  • a) Auxiliary to vital organs (Khidmat-e-muhaeya): Lungs (serves Heart), Liver/Stomach (serves vitality).
  • b) Instruments of their functions (Khidmat-e-moodeya): Arteries, Nerves, Veins.

11 Primary Human Body Systems

There are 11 main systems that keep our bodies functioning. Learn the primary roles of each system as defined in modern anatomy and natural medicine.

01

Endocrine System

Regulation of body processes through hormone production and chemical messaging.

02

Nervous System

Processing center for sensory input, using input to elicit appropriate physiological responses.

03

Respiratory System

Gas exchange between internal organs and the external environment.

04

Cardiovascular System

Circulation of blood, which transports gases, nutrients, hormones, and wastes throughout tissues.

05

Digestive System

Physical and chemical breakdown of food to allow absorption of nutrients.

06

Lymphatic System

Circulation of lymph fluid, which maintains fluid balance and helps fight systemic infection.

07

Reproductive System

Production of specialized reproductive cells that will generate offspring.

08

Urinary System

Filtration of blood metabolic waste and excretion of fluids from the body.

09

Integumentary System

Protection against external environment and thermal regulation of body temperature.

10

Muscular System

Executes voluntary physical locomotion and involuntary muscular movements.

11

Skeletal Support

Structural framework providing physical support and vital protection of internal organs.
